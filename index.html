01 / Introduction

Modernizing the Power Grid

Electric power substations are the critical nodes of our electricity distribution infrastructure — converting, regulating, and transmitting power across vast networks. Yet despite their vital importance, most substations in developing regions still rely on manual, periodic inspections to detect faults, monitor transformer health, and log operational parameters.

This project introduces a fully integrated, IoT-based smart monitoring system purpose-built for distribution substations. At its core lies the ESP32 microcontroller — a dual-core, Wi-Fi-enabled processing unit that continuously reads data from a carefully designed sensor network and transmits it to a remote cloud dashboard in real time.

The sensor network spans three critical dimensions of substation health:

  • 🌡 PT100 Temperature Sensor — monitors transformer oil and winding temperatures against IS-standard thresholds (35°C oil / 40°C winding rise).
  • ⚡ ACS712 Current Sensor — detects overload, fault currents, and phase imbalances through Hall-effect measurement, mirroring industrial 500/5A CTs.
  • 📡 HC-SR04 Ultrasonic Sensor — remotely measures transformer oil conservator levels, replacing the manual sight glass with a digital metric.
  • 💡 LDR Oil Quality Sensor — gauges oil clarity degradation, providing early warnings of dielectric fluid contamination.

All parameters are displayed live on a 16×2 LCD at the hardware level and simultaneously streamed to a web/mobile IoT dashboard, enabling operators to supervise and control the substation from any location with internet access.

This system was developed as the final-year project for the Department of Electronics & Telecommunication Engineering at R.H. Sapat College of Engineering, Management Studies and Research, Nashik — with field research grounded in an industrial visit to Koyna Engineers Pvt. Ltd., a premier transformer manufacturer in MIDC Satpur, Nashik.

02 / Problem Statement

The Critical Gap in Substation Management

Traditional substation monitoring is slow, costly, and dangerously reactive — problems that an IoT solution directly addresses.

01

Reactive Fault Detection

Conventional substations have no real-time fault detection. Overloads, winding short circuits, and insulation failures are discovered only after catastrophic equipment damage occurs or scheduled human inspection takes place — sometimes hours or days after a fault develops. This reactive approach results in unplanned outages, costly emergency repairs, and reduced transformer lifespan.

02

Manual Oil Level Monitoring

Transformer dielectric oil — the critical insulating and cooling medium — can gradually leak undetected. Operators rely on manual sight glass readings during periodic walkthroughs. In remote or unmanned substations, a slow oil leak can progress from "low" to "critically low," exposing windings, causing insulation breakdown, and leading to catastrophic transformer failure worth crores in replacement costs.

03

Temperature Threshold Violations

Industrial transformers operate within strict IS-standard temperature limits (35°C oil temperature rise, 40°C winding rise above 40°C ambient). Without continuous monitoring, thermal runaway from sustained overloads or cooling system failures can remain undetected. Sustained over-temperature conditions accelerate insulation aging logarithmically — cutting transformer life by 50% for every 6–8°C above the rated limit.

04

High Operational Cost & Safety Risks

Manual inspection of high-voltage substations requires trained personnel to physically enter energized switchyards — an inherently hazardous activity. The operational cost of maintaining inspection schedules, the human safety risk of proximity to 11kV and 33kV live equipment, and the inability to monitor remote or rural substations effectively represent major systemic vulnerabilities in the current power distribution model.

03 / Objectives

What We Set Out to Build

Five engineering objectives were defined to guide this project from field research to a fully functional, tested prototype.

1

Implement Real-Time Multi-Parameter Monitoring

Design and deploy a sensor network capable of continuously measuring all four critical substation parameters — transformer oil temperature (PT100), load current (ACS712), transformer oil conservator level (HC-SR04 ultrasonic), and oil quality/clarity (LDR) — with a sampling rate sufficient for real-time fault detection and an accuracy that meets industrial calibration standards.

2

Enable Automated Equipment Protection

Develop and code threshold-based protection logic on the ESP32 microcontroller that automatically isolates faulty circuits the instant a sensor reading breaches pre-programmed safety limits. The protection module uses a 5V opto-isolated relay to trip a Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB), eliminating human reaction time from the fault-clearing equation entirely and achieving protection response in milliseconds.

3

Develop a Remote Supervision Dashboard

Build a cloud-connected web and mobile application that receives live sensor telemetry from the ESP32 via Wi-Fi and presents it in a clean, intuitive dashboard. Operators must be able to view all four parameters in real time, review historical logs, receive push notifications on parameter threshold breaches, and remotely command the relay module to trip or re-energize a circuit — all from any internet-connected device.

4

Ground Design in Real Industrial Benchmarks

Validate the system's sensor choices, threshold limits, and protection logic against real-world industrial specifications obtained during the field visit to Koyna Engineers Pvt. Ltd. This includes mapping the ACS712's scaled current output to the 500/5A CT ratio of the 33kV metering cubicle and setting oil temperature thresholds based on IS 335:2018 standard limits observed on the 630 kVA transformer nameplate.

5

Fabricate a Functional PCB Prototype

Design and fabricate a custom single-layer FR4 PCB integrating the power supply section (230V AC to 12V/5V regulated DC), signal conditioning circuits (Wheatstone bridge + Op-Amp for PT100, voltage divider for LDR), microcontroller section (ESP32), and sensor/IoT communication interfaces — with proper grounding, decoupling capacitors, and isolation between AC and DC domains. Validate all hardware through systematic testing protocols.

04 / Related Work

Standing on the Shoulders of Prior Research

A thorough literature survey of published IoT and power systems research shaped our system architecture and validated our sensor choices.

2023

IEEE ICPS — IoT Real-Time Monitoring for Distribution Substations

Talha et al. demonstrated a Wi-Fi-based monitoring system for distribution substations using STM32 and MQTT protocol. Their work established the viability of microcontroller-driven telemetry for substation data but lacked integrated automated trip mechanisms. Our project extends this by adding ACS712-triggered relay protection and oil level monitoring as additional parameters.

2025

IRJMETS — IoT Substation Monitoring & Controlling

Sahebrao et al. proposed an Arduino Uno-based substation monitoring system with temperature and current sensors. While effective as a proof of concept, the Arduino platform lacks native Wi-Fi and the processing power for simultaneous multi-sensor acquisition and IoT communication. This gap directly motivated our selection of the dual-core ESP32, which handles sensor reading and cloud communication on separate cores without data latency.

Industrial

Field Research — Koyna Engineers Pvt. Ltd., Nashik

First-hand observation of a fully assembled 630 kVA, 11kV/433V Dyn11 distribution transformer, a 33kV metering cubicle (500/5A CT ratio), ONAN cooling systems, vacuum-treated dielectric oil, and High-Pot dielectric testers provided the engineering benchmarks that pure literature surveys cannot. Our sensor thresholds, protection relay logic, and oil monitoring concept are directly derived from observing the manual limitations of these real industrial systems.

Standard

IS 335:2018 — Mineral Insulating Oils for Transformers

The Bureau of Indian Standards specification IS 335:2018 defines the dielectric breakdown voltage thresholds, moisture content limits, and flash point requirements for transformer mineral insulating oils. Our system's LDR-based oil quality sensor and PT100 temperature thresholds (35°C oil rise, 40°C winding rise above 40°C ambient) are calibrated to trigger alerts based on deviation from parameters implied by this standard, ensuring our protection logic is grounded in recognized Indian engineering norms.

05 / Expected Outcomes

What This System Delivers

Verified through systematic hardware and software testing, the prototype demonstrates these measurable outcomes.

📊

Real-Time Parameter Visibility

All four substation parameters — oil temperature, load current, oil tank level, and oil quality index — are displayed on the 16×2 LCD at the hardware level and simultaneously streamed to the IoT cloud dashboard. Operators gain a continuous, live picture of substation health without stepping foot inside the switchyard. System testing confirmed data transmission latency of under 2 seconds from sensor reading to cloud update under standard Wi-Fi conditions.

🛡

Automated Fault Isolation & Trip Response

When any parameter breaches its configured threshold — tested with controlled overload current, heated oil, and manually reduced oil level during the hardware testing phase — the ESP32 instantly commands the relay module to trip the MCB. Testing confirmed the automated trip response within milliseconds of threshold breach, with zero false positives in the overload test and correct fault identification across all four simulated fault scenarios.

🔔

Instant Remote Fault Alerts

Upon fault detection, the system simultaneously triggers a local buzzer alarm and dispatches a real-time push notification to the operator's registered devices via the IoT platform. This dual-channel alert architecture ensures that no fault goes unnoticed regardless of whether the operator is on-site or hundreds of kilometres away, dramatically reducing Mean Time to Repair (MTTR) for substation faults.

📈

Predictive Maintenance Foundation

By logging all sensor readings to the cloud over time, the system builds historical trend data that enables predictive — rather than reactive — maintenance. A gradual upward drift in oil temperature over weeks, or a slow decline in oil level indicating a minor leak, can be identified well before reaching critical thresholds. This predictive capability is the most economically significant outcome of the project, with the potential to extend transformer service life and eliminate unplanned outage costs.

💡

Cost-Effective Scalable Architecture

The total component cost of the prototype hardware (ESP32, sensors, custom PCB, relay, LCD, power supply) is a fraction of traditional SCADA systems, making this solution viable for deployment at low-voltage and medium-voltage substations in rural or semi-urban distribution networks where full SCADA infrastructure is economically unfeasible. The ESP32's built-in Wi-Fi eliminates the need for a separate communication modem, further reducing hardware costs.

06 / Future Scope

The Road Beyond the Prototype

The current prototype establishes a solid foundation. These are the natural next steps for industrial deployment and research extension.

🤖

AI-Powered Predictive Analytics

Integrating machine learning models trained on historical sensor data to predict equipment failure before it occurs. Pattern recognition on temperature trends and current waveform harmonics could provide early warnings days or weeks ahead of a fault — moving the system from threshold-based protection to truly proactive AI-driven grid intelligence.

⚙

Motorized Automatic Tap Changer

The current 630 kVA transformer uses a manual off-circuit tap changer requiring de-energization for voltage regulation. A future iteration of this IoT system could continuously monitor input voltage and automatically command a motorized on-load tap changer (OLTC) to maintain output voltage within ±2% of nominal — a key step toward a truly smart, self-regulating grid node.

🌐

LoRaWAN & 4G/5G Connectivity

The current prototype relies on standard Wi-Fi, limiting deployment to areas with existing infrastructure. Replacing the ESP32's Wi-Fi stack with LoRaWAN for long-range, low-power communication or integrating a 4G/5G cellular module would enable deployment in completely remote, off-grid substations — expanding the system's applicability to rural electrification infrastructure across India.

🔗

SCADA System Integration via Modbus/DNP3

For large utilities managing hundreds of substations, the system could be extended to communicate with existing SCADA platforms using industrial protocols like Modbus TCP or DNP3. This would allow IoT-monitored substations to feed real-time data directly into utility-level Energy Management Systems (EMS), providing a bridge between low-cost IoT hardware and enterprise-grade grid management software.

📱

Dedicated Mobile Application

Developing a purpose-built Android/iOS application with biometric authentication, customizable alert thresholds per substation, offline data caching, and a multi-substation map view would make the system production-ready for utility deployment. The app could also include an AR feature to overlay live sensor readings onto a camera view of the physical switchyard for field technicians.

🛡

Cybersecurity Hardening

As substations become networked nodes, they become targets for cyberattacks. Future work must include TLS/SSL encryption for all cloud communication, device authentication certificates, intrusion detection at the firmware level, and compliance with IEC 62351 — the international standard for securing power systems communication — before any utility-scale deployment can responsibly proceed.

09 / Conclusion

A Smarter Grid Starts Here

This project successfully demonstrates that modern, affordable IoT technology can fundamentally transform the way power distribution substations are monitored and protected. By integrating embedded hardware, a multi-parameter sensor network, Wi-Fi-based IoT communication, and automated protection logic into a single cohesive system, the project has built a working proof-of-concept that directly addresses the most critical shortcomings of traditional, manual substation management.

The system was not designed in a vacuum. Every hardware choice — from the PT100's temperature thresholds to the ACS712's current scaling — was grounded in real-world industrial specifications observed during the field visit to Koyna Engineers Pvt. Ltd. This hands-on industrial validation transforms the project from a theoretical exercise into a practically motivated engineering solution with clear deployment pathways.

The most significant contribution of this work is the demonstration that a system costing a small fraction of conventional SCADA infrastructure can deliver comparable core functionality — real-time monitoring, automated fault isolation, remote supervision, and predictive maintenance data collection — making advanced substation intelligence accessible to utilities serving rural, semi-urban, and developing-region power networks.

All four hardware test scenarios — temperature fault, oil level depletion, oil quality degradation, and current overload — were successfully detected and automatically tripped within expected parameters. The IoT dashboard operates reliably with sub-2-second data refresh, and remote relay control was verified across network conditions. The prototype has met every objective defined at the project's inception.

With the foundation built, the path forward is clear: extend to LoRaWAN for remote deployment, integrate AI-based predictive analytics on accumulated cloud data, and harden the communication stack to IEC 62351 cybersecurity standards. This system is not merely a final-year project — it is a scalable platform for the next generation of India's smart power grid.
